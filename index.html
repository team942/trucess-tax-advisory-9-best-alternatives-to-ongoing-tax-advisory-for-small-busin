If you run a small business and you've been paying a retainer for ongoing tax advisory, you've probably wondered whether that monthly invoice is still earning its keep. This guide breaks down nine alternatives — from specialized tax-strategy firms to software, hybrid services, and Big Four advisors — that help owner-operators and finance leads plan smarter, cut liability, and file with confidence in 2026.

What to Consider

1. Buyer fit. A single-member LLC with $80K in profit has completely different needs than an S-corp doing $4M with inventory in three states. Before you evaluate anything, be honest about your entity structure, revenue, and whether your pain is filing, planning, or strategy. Ongoing advisory makes sense for some; a project-based engagement or software platform makes more sense for most.

2. Core capabilities. Look for what you actually need: quarterly estimated tax calculations, depreciation and cost segregation strategy, R&D credits, multi-state nexus handling, entity restructuring advice, and clean year-end packages your CPA can file from. Planning without execution support — projections you can't act on — is where cheap alternatives fail.

3. Evidence and results. Ask for concrete outcomes: average tax savings documented in case studies, average depreciation acceleration on cost segregation engagements, and whether the firm stands behind positions if the IRS asks questions. Reputable providers will quantify their value; vague promises of "savings" are a red flag.

4. Ease of use. How much work falls on you? Software-only options demand that you learn the tool; done-for-you firms need document uploads and a discovery call. The best middle ground is a service that pulls from your books (QuickBooks, Xero) so you're not re-entering data every quarter.

5. Price and value. Compare the all-in cost — software subscription, filing fees, per-engagement pricing, or retainer — against realistic savings. A $7,500 cost segregation study that frees $90,000 in first-year cash flow is a bargain; a $300/mo retainer that produces one planning call a year is not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ongoing tax advisory actually worth it for a small business?

For businesses with simple structures and stable income, usually not — a strong annual planning engagement plus good filing software covers 90% of the value at a fraction of the retainer cost. Ongoing advisory earns its price when you have frequent transactions, entity changes, or significant depreciable assets requiring timing decisions.

What's the difference between tax preparation and tax planning?

Preparation documents what already happened; planning changes what happens before year-end. Depreciation strategy, entity selection, retirement contributions, and income timing are planning decisions — and they're worthless after December 31. That's why the best alternatives on this list emphasize strategy, not just filing.

Can software replace a tax advisor entirely?

For straightforward returns, yes. TurboTax and TaxSlayer handle compliance well. But software can't spot a cost segregation opportunity in your building purchase or restructure your entity mid-year — that takes a human who knows the rules and your numbers.

When does it make sense to move from a small firm to a Big Four advisor?

Generally when you hit international operations, M&A activity, or revenue where the tax complexity itself justifies enterprise fees — typically well past $10M. Until then, specialty boutiques and mid-market firms deliver comparable technical work at a fraction of the cost.
